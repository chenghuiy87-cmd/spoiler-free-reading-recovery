A field guide for physical-book readers

Return to the page without restarting the book.

A 60-second restart card and a no-quotes discussion worksheet. Both preserve your reaction—not the author’s passage—so they stay useful without creating spoilers.

Tool 01 · interrupted reading

The 60-second restart card

Use this when you have been away from a physical book long enough to remember the page but not the thought that made you stop.

  1. Find the last page you remember. Do not reread a whole chapter yet.
  2. Name the reason. Choose Keep, Felt, Clue, or Discuss.
  3. Rate the pull. Use 1 for faint, 2 for clear, or 3 for strong.
  4. Write one return cue in your own words. Keep it short enough to scan before reading.
  5. Read two pages forward. Then decide whether context has returned.

Page ___ · Keep / Felt / Clue / Discuss · 1 / 2 / 3
Return cue: ______________________________

Boundary: record your reaction, question, or inference. Do not copy the passage and do not summarize events beyond that page.

Tool 02 · book-club preparation

The no-quotes discussion worksheet

Choose no more than five moments. A page number plus your reaction is usually enough to recover a useful discussion without highlighting half the book.

Turn each reaction into a question

  • Keep: What idea here could travel beyond this book?
  • Felt: What changed the emotional temperature?
  • Clue: What did you suspect when you reached this page?
  • Discuss: Which choice, claim, or tension deserves another reader’s view?

Three-page agenda: start with the strongest reaction, follow with a different reaction type, and finish with a page where the group disagrees.

Spoiler-light rule: announce the page and question first. If someone has not reached it, skip the question without explaining what happens there.
