Spoiler-light reading tool

The sensory-motif tracker for close reading

A recurring smell, texture, sound, taste, or color can carry a scene's pressure before the story explains it. This tracker follows the sensory change without copying prose.

Build the card

Save one page where this method becomes useful. Complete the fields in your own words so the page remains the route back to the source.

FieldPrompt
SenseWhich sensory channel is active?
ObjectWhat carries the sensation?
ContextWho notices it and under what pressure?
ChangeHow is this occurrence different from the last?

Run the check

  • Record the sensation in your own words.
  • Separate what the character senses from what you associate with it.
  • Look for a change in intensity, source, or emotional direction.

Working sentence: My current reading is ______________________________.

Review rule

At the third occurrence, decide whether the motif connects scenes, characters, memory, or threat.

Boundaries

This is a personal reflection aid, not a quotation archive, plot substitute, academic citation, factual verification, or proof of an author's intention. Return to the full passage before making consequential claims.
