The epigraph-connection note for chapter openings

An epigraph can set a lens, counterpoint, or misleading expectation. This note delays interpretation until the chapter gives something concrete to compare.

Build the card

Save one page where this method becomes useful. Complete the fields in your own words so the page remains the route back to the source.

FieldPrompt
SourceWho or what is credited?
Surface ideaWhat broad idea appears without quoting it?
Chapter echoWhich scene, image, or argument connects?
RelationshipLens, contrast, warning, joke, or unresolved?

Run the check

  • Do not force a connection in the first paragraph.
  • Compare the epigraph's speaker with the chapter's viewpoint.
  • Allow the relationship to remain provisional.

Working sentence: My current reading is ______________________________.

Review rule

At the chapter end, keep, revise, or discard the connection and name the page that changed your view.

Boundaries

This is a personal reflection aid, not a quotation archive, plot substitute, academic citation, factual verification, or proof of an author's intention. Return to the full passage before making consequential claims.
